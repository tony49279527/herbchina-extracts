Overview

What it is, and how we handle it.

Betaine, also called trimethylglycine, is a small zwitterionic molecule traded as a white crystalline or granular powder. Sports-nutrition buyers specify it for pre-workout, intra-workout and recovery-oriented powder systems, where flow, solubility and taste neutrality matter as much as assay. FAQ

Common questions.

Should we specify anhydrous betaine or the hydrochloride form, and how does that change the spec?
They are different materials for different purposes: anhydrous betaine is the trimethylglycine form, while betaine HCl is a salt used where an acidic matrix is intended. They are not interchangeable on a label or in a formulation, and assay methods and molecular weights differ. Tell us which form your formulation calls for and we will match against what the partner producer can realistically supply.
What particle form is available, and how does it affect blend flow and solubility?
Betaine is commonly traded as crystalline or agglomerated granular powder, and the two behave differently in ribbon blenders and high-speed mixers — granulation tends to flow and dissolve better, fine crystal often costs less. Sieve range or granulation size is a legitimate spec point. Send the mesh or flow characteristic your line needs and we will confirm what can be held.
How is assay verified, and can documentation be arranged for our QA file?
Assay is usually confirmed by titration or HPLC depending on the buyer's requirement, and moisture, heavy metals and microbial parameters are common additional line items. The testing scope is agreed per buyer specification, and a COA and TDS can be arranged on request once your target specification is final.
