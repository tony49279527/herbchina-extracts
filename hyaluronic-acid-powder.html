Buying guide

Buying checkpoints for Hyaluronic Acid Powder.

Sodium hyaluronate is sold into two main application tracks, cosmetic and food grade, and the grade you buy should be matched to where the material will actually go. Confirm on the COA which grade is being offered and what the accompanying documentation covers, because a cosmetic-grade lot may not carry the same testing package a food or nutraceutical buyer needs. Fermentation-derived material is the norm, and you should be able to see the route stated clearly rather than inferred.

Beyond grade, the specification that most affects your formulation is molecular weight, and suppliers typically quote a range rather than a single figure. Ask how the range is determined and whether it is measured on the incoming material or on the finished lot. Because sodium hyaluronate is hygroscopic, appearance and flow can shift with moisture uptake, so a white to off-white fine powder that has clumped in the bag is a handling flag rather than a spec failure on its own.

Storage follows directly from that hygroscopicity. Keep containers closed, dry, and at moderate temperature, and open only what you can use or reseal properly. When you first qualify a supplier, request a small sample so you can test dissolution behaviour in your own system before committing to a bulk format.

FAQ

Common questions.

How is the molecular weight of your sodium hyaluronate specified, and can it be chosen?
Molecular weight is typically quoted as a range (for example low, medium or high molecular weight fractions). Buyers usually state the range their formulation targets, and we confirm which ranges the partner producer can offer against that request. Assay and molecular weight are normally verified by HPLC or viscometry methods; the exact test method is agreed per buyer specification.
Is the material fermentation-derived or animal-derived, and how is that documented?
The grades we quote are fermentation-derived (microbial) sodium hyaluronate. Origin and production route are stated on the producer documentation, and buyers who need confirmation of the fermentation route can request it as part of the agreed testing scope. We do not stock animal-derived material as a standard option.
What spec details do you need from us to check feasibility and arrange COA/TDS?
Send your target assay, molecular weight range, grade (cosmetic vs. food/nutraceutical), particle form and any limit tests. COA and TDS can be arranged on request; testing scope is agreed per buyer specification. We then confirm feasibility with the partner producer honestly rather than assuming availability.
