White Tea Extract

Camellia sinensis (white tea) — a young-leaf and bud tea extract supplied for premium beverage and antioxidant-positioned formulations, distinct from green tea extract.

Overview

What it is, and how we handle it.

White tea extract is produced from young leaves and buds of Camellia sinensis, harvested and minimally processed before drying, which preserves a lighter flavor and a naturally lower caffeine level than green tea. Buyers specify tea polyphenols as the primary marker. It is explicitly distinct from green tea extract — different leaf grade, different processing. FAQ

Common questions.

What actually separates white tea extract from green tea extract in terms of raw material and process?
White tea is made from young leaves and unopened buds picked early and simply withered and dried, with minimal rolling or heat treatment; green tea typically uses more mature leaf and undergoes pan-firing or steaming to halt oxidation. That difference carries into the extract: white tea extract tends to be lighter in color and flavor with naturally lower caffeine, and it should not be swapped for green tea extract on a label.
How do you verify caffeine is genuinely lower, and can that be reported?
Caffeine is normally quantified by HPLC alongside the polyphenol assay, so it can be reported as a separate line item rather than inferred. Because caffeine level varies with leaf grade and season, ask for it explicitly in your spec. The testing scope is agreed per buyer specification and documentation can be arranged on request.
Which polyphenol assay should we specify, and does it distinguish white tea from green tea?
Total tea polyphenols by UV is the most commonly requested figure; buyers wanting more resolution add individual catechin profiling by HPLC. Total polyphenols alone will not distinguish white from green tea, so if authenticity matters to your claim, ask for a catechin profile or fingerprint as well.
