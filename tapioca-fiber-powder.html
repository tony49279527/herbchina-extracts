FAQ

Common questions.

What is tapioca fiber, and how does it differ from tapioca starch?
Tapioca fiber is a soluble fiber produced by enzymatic conversion of cassava (Manihot esculenta) starch, yielding a material that is largely indigestible. It is not the same as native tapioca starch, which is a digestible carbohydrate. Buyers should specify fiber content rather than starch content when defining the grade.
What fiber content grades are commonly requested?
Market grades are usually quoted on soluble fiber content on a dry basis, with sugar content often specified alongside for low-sugar formulation work. Solubility and solution clarity may also matter for beverage applications. We confirm the achievable range against your target specification.
Can sugar content and fiber content be verified before shipment?
COA and TDS can be arranged on request; testing scope is agreed per buyer specification. Provide the parameters and methods you need — fiber, sugar, moisture or others — and we will confirm what the partner producer can document.
