Sage Extract

Salvia officinalis leaf extract in ratio or rosmarinic acid grades — a versatile botanical for cognitive-focus, culinary, and cosmetic formulations.

Overview

What it is, and how we handle it.

Sage extract comes from the leaf of Salvia officinalis and is traded in two broad directions: ratio extracts such as 10:1, and standardized grades based on rosmarinic acid. It appears in cognitive-focus supplement concepts as well as culinary and cosmetic applications. As a Xi'an-based trading company working through vetted partner producers, we help buyers match the right grade to their intended formulation and confirm documentation feasibility before quoting.

FAQ

Common questions.

Should we specify a ratio extract or a rosmarinic acid grade?
It depends on your formulation and label. Ratio extracts such as 10:1 are common where the ingredient is positioned traditionally, while rosmarinic acid grades are requested when a measurable marker is needed. Some buyers ask for both a ratio and a marker figure. Tell us which direction you prefer and we will confirm what is feasible.
Is the extract standardized on any other compounds?
Rosmarinic acid is the most commonly requested marker for sage, though some buyers ask about total phenolic content or other measured values. If you have a specific marker or method in mind, share it and we will check with the partner producer rather than assume.
What documentation is available for spec review?
A COA and TDS can be arranged on request, with testing scope agreed per buyer specification. Provide your target grade and any limits, and we will confirm what can be supplied with the material.
