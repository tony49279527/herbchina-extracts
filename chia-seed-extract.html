FAQ

Common questions.

How are omega-3 ALA and fiber content commonly reported for chia seed material?
ALA is generally reported as a percentage of total fatty acids or as total oil content, depending on the analytical method, and fiber is often split between soluble and insoluble fractions. Because reporting conventions vary, the basis should be stated clearly in your specification. We check your stated targets against what partner producers can support.
Is the material a full-fat milled seed or a concentrated extract, and how does that affect the specification?
Both forms trade: milled or ground chia seed carries the natural oil and fiber matrix, while a more concentrated extract raises the ALA or fiber figure per gram. The form you choose changes handling properties, flow and shelf-life behavior in finished products. Tell us which form your formulation requires and we will confirm realistic options.
Can a COA and TDS be arranged for chia seed extract?
A COA and TDS can be arranged on request; testing scope is agreed per buyer specification so the documents cover the ALA, fiber or other parameters you require. Share your target figures and method preferences and we will confirm which can be documented before ordering.
