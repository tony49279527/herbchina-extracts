Overview

What it is, and how we handle it.

Inulin is a fructan extracted from chicory root (Cichorium intybus) and traded as a white powder, commonly at 90%+ inulin content, in native and short-chain forms. It is a staple in prebiotic-fiber and gut-comfort product concepts. FAQ

Common questions.

What is the difference between native inulin and short-chain inulin for formulation use?
Native inulin has a longer average chain length and is often chosen for food matrices and powder blends, while short-chain grades (oligofructose-enriched) are more soluble and commonly used where a sweeter, more dispersible fiber is wanted. Inulin content and chain-length profile are the key comparison points, and both are agreed per buyer specification.
What plant part is your inulin sourced from, and how is inulin content measured?
The starting material is chicory root (Cichorium intybus). Inulin content is typically measured by enzymatic or HPLC methods, and results are reported against the agreed specification. The exact test method is agreed per buyer specification rather than assumed.
What do you need to arrange COA/TDS and check feasibility for a specific inulin spec?
Share your target inulin content, chain profile, mesh size, moisture and any additional tests such as ash or heavy metals. COA and TDS can be arranged on request; testing scope is agreed per buyer specification. We then verify feasibility with the vetted partner producer honestly.
