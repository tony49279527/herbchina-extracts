Amaranth — seed-derived extract for functional-food and nutrition formulations.

Overview

What it is, and how we handle it.

Amaranth extract is derived from the seed of Amaranthus hypochondriacus and is valued in formulation work for its squalene content, with protein-focused grades also traded for nutrition applications. Buyers in functional food, nutrition powders and cosmetic-adjacent categories request it as a plant-derived alternative to other squalene sources. Specification interest centers on squalene concentration and, where relevant, protein level. We source through vetted partner producers and confirm feasibility against your target spec.

FAQ

Common questions.

Is amaranth squalene usually supplied as an oil or as a powder?
Both forms are traded, and the right one depends on your dosage form. Squalene-rich amaranth oil suits softgel, emulsion and cosmetic applications, while a powder format is typically requested when the ingredient must be dry-blended into nutrition or functional-food matrices. If you need a powder, specify whether the squalene is carried on a solid support and at what concentration.
How do buyers verify squalene content and screen for adulteration?
Squalene is commonly quantified by GC or HPLC, sometimes with a fatty-acid profile run alongside it for identity confirmation. Because amaranth squalene is often compared with shark-derived or olive-derived material, buyers sometimes request origin-relevant markers or stable-isotope work. Specify the method and any identity checks you require so the partner producer's capability can be confirmed upfront.
What documents come with an amaranth extract quotation?
COA and TDS can be arranged on request; testing scope is agreed per buyer specification. Share your target squalene or protein figure, preferred method and any restricted-solvent requirements, and we will confirm what can be supported before you commit.
