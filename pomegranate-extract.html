Overview

What it is, and how we handle it.

Pomegranate extract is derived from the fruit peel of Punica granatum and carries punicalagins and ellagic acid as its headline markers. Demand keeps building in beauty-from-within and antioxidant-positioned supplements, where formulators want a consistent marker profile rather than raw fruit powder. FAQ

Common questions.

Do your pomegranate extract grades specify punicalagins, ellagic acid, or both?
Both marker routes are commonly requested. Some buyers standardize on punicalagins by HPLC, others on ellagic acid, and some ask for a combined profile. Tell us which marker governs your label and we will confirm what our partner producers can consistently deliver.
Is the extract made from whole fruit or peel only, and does the plant part affect the marker profile?
Peel is the usual raw material because that is where punicalagins concentrate. Whole-fruit inputs tend to dilute the marker and introduce more sugar and fiber. We ask partner producers to state the plant part and can discuss how peel-only sourcing aligns with your specification.
How do you handle COA and TDS for a pomegranate extract order?
COA and TDS can be arranged on request, with testing scope agreed per buyer specification. Share your marker targets, method preferences and any identity checks you require, and we will confirm feasibility through our vetted partner producers before you commit.
