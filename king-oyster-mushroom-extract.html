Overview

What it is, and how we handle it.

King oyster mushroom extract is produced from the fruiting body of Pleurotus eryngii and supplied as a light tan powder, standardised on polysaccharides and beta-glucan. Brands request it for immune-positioned supplements and for gourmet-nutrition and savoury flavour concepts, where its umami character is an advantage. It is a different species from common oyster mushroom (Pleurotus ostreatus). We source it through vetted partner producers from our Xi'an base.

FAQ

Common questions.

How does this differ from common oyster mushroom extract?
This product is Pleurotus eryngii, king oyster mushroom, which forms a thick stem and is prized for texture and umami. It is a different species from Pleurotus ostreatus, the common oyster mushroom, and the two should not be substituted for one another. Buyers commonly require the species to be stated on documentation; we confirm species and plant part with the partner producer for each enquiry.
Is the extract made from fruiting body or mycelium, and how are polysaccharides measured?
This material is fruiting-body derived. Mycelium-on-grain products are widely traded in the mushroom category and are often confused with fruiting-body extracts, so buyers commonly specify the source explicitly and check for grain or starch residues. Polysaccharides are typically measured by UV-Vis, with beta-glucan reported separately by an enzymatic or comparable method; the panel is agreed per buyer specification.
What polysaccharide level and beta-glucan figure can realistically be requested?
Standardised polysaccharide grades commonly requested sit between about 20% and 50%, with beta-glucan reported on its own basis since the two figures do not track each other directly. Higher polysaccharide claims are often achieved with carrier or by different measurement methods, so it is worth agreeing the method alongside the target.
