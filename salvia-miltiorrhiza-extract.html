Salvia Miltiorrhiza Extract

Salvia miltiorrhiza — the danshen root, positioned around cardiovascular-oriented formulations.

Overview

What it is, and how we handle it.

Salvia miltiorrhiza extract comes from the danshen root and carries two distinct marker families: the lipophilic tanshinones and the water-soluble salvianolic acids, with salvianolic acid B most often referenced. Formulators working on cardiovascular-oriented botanical blends ask for it as a multi-marker ingredient. We source through vetted partner producers as a Xi'an-based trading company.

FAQ

Common questions.

Why does Salvia miltiorrhiza extract show two very different marker groups?
The root contains both lipophilic tanshinones and water-soluble salvianolic acids, and extraction route determines which dominates. Alcohol-based extraction favors tanshinones; water-based favors salvianolic acids. Buyers therefore need to state which family governs their specification rather than assuming a single number covers both.
Can one extract be standardized on both tanshinones and salvianolic acid B?
It is possible, but the two markers pull in different directions and the ratio depends on the extraction process used. If your specification requires both, tell us the individual targets and we will confirm what partner producers can realistically deliver rather than promising a combined figure upfront.
How are COA and TDS handled for Salvia miltiorrhiza extract?
COA and TDS can be arranged on request, with testing scope agreed per buyer specification. Because this extract is often evaluated against more than one marker, buyers frequently specify which HPLC methods they expect — send those details and we will confirm feasibility with our vetted partner producers.
