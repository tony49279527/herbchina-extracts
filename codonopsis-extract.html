FAQ

Common questions.

Is a 10:1 ratio or a polysaccharide-standardized grade the better fit for a blend?
It depends on how your label and QC are set up. Ratio extracts are simple to declare and easy to blend, while polysaccharide-standardized grades give a measurable marker for specification and release testing. Some buyers carry both. Tell us which basis your formulation and labeling use and we will confirm what the partner producer can realistically hold.
Which plant part is used and how does root grade affect color and solubility?
The root is the material used, and root age, grade and post-harvest handling influence final color, ash and water solubility. Higher-ratio extracts tend to be darker and slightly less soluble, which matters in clear beverage applications. If appearance or solubility is critical, state the parameter and we will confirm what is achievable.
How is adulteration typically checked for a root extract like this?
Common checkpoints include TLC or HPLC fingerprinting against a reference, marker content where a standardized grade is claimed, and ratio verification by extract yield logic. Because ratio claims are easy to overstate, buyers often ask for a marker-assisted release. Send the verification approach your QA expects and the testing scope can be agreed per buyer specification.
