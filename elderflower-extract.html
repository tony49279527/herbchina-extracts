What it is, and how we handle it.

FAQ

Common questions.

Which plant part is used for the extract, and does it affect the flavonoid profile?
Standard elderflower extract is produced from the dried flowers of Sambucus nigra rather than the berries or stems. Flower-derived material typically carries a lighter color and a different flavonoid pattern than berry extracts, which matters if your formulation is benchmarked against a floral reference. Specify the plant part in your target spec so feasibility and analytical expectations can be aligned before sampling.
How is total flavonoid content usually tested, and what grades do buyers ask for?
Total flavonoids are commonly quantified by UV spectrophotometry against a reference standard such as rutin or quercetin, though HPLC-based marker methods are also used for more specific profiling. Buyers frequently request concentrations in the range of a few percent up to more concentrated grades, expressed as total flavonoids. Because test method and standard choice change the reported number, agree the method with your lab before we confirm feasibility.
Can you arrange a COA and technical data sheet for a specified elderflower grade?
COA and TDS can be arranged on request; testing scope is agreed per buyer specification. Share your target marker, method preference and any restricted-substance requirements, and we will confirm what the partner producer can realistically support before documentation is issued.
